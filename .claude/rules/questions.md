# Questions to Eric

**A question to Eric must be answerable without scrolling back.** It carries:

- what the thing is, in plain language;
- the facts that bear on it;
- each option, spelled out;
- your recommendation.

Or it links one GitHub comment that carries all of that.

- **Never re-ask with a bare label** ("A or B?", "card fit?", "lab#54?"). Re-ask with the link and a one-line restatement.
- **Put the question last** in the message, after any report of tool work. Never bury it in the middle.
- **Keep open decisions in one place.** A decision that stays open across turns goes in one tracked list, such as one issue body edited in place. It comes off the list once decided.
- **A short summary must be self-contained too.** Every line stands alone: say what the thing is, not what it's called. "lab#54" becomes "Haru's gestures have never played on the real robot." Name the artifact after that statement, and only if Eric needs to find it. A summary made of pointers is worse than the long version: it keeps the context dependency and drops the evidence.

**Why (Eric, 2026-10-07):** a seat asked "card fit A or B?" for a day without restating it. Eric: "I DON'T KNOW WHAT A OR B MEANS. ... I have to scroll through chat history ... Half the time I can't find it ... your message with the question is lost." And after a short summary made of pointers: "When I say this is way too much information and ask for a DOTS-KISS explanation and you give a bunch of context-dependent references to stuff you didn't explain in the DOTS-KISS summary, YOU HAVE FAILED." Brevity serves his attention budget; a pointer costs him a lookup.
